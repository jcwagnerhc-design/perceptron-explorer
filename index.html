What to notice

Adelie vs Gentoo. The mistake count falls to zero and training stops on its own. Now reset, change the learning rate, and run it again. The three numbers come out ten or a hundred times bigger — but the line does not move at all. Every weight scaled by the same amount is the same line, because only the ratio between them sets its angle. Worth thinking about before you read the explanation.
Adelie vs Chinstrap. The mistake count drops fast, then bounces around a handful of penguins and never reaches zero — even though that handful is only about 2% of the data. This is not a bug and it is not under-training. Look at the plot: a few Adelies sit inside the Chinstrap crowd, and no single straight line can get around them. Every time the line moves to catch one, another penguin falls out the far side, so the rule never runs out of work.

One neuron draws one line. To carve out a shape that is not a line, you need a layer of neurons feeding into another layer — a network.

Data: Palmer Station Antarctica LTER / Dr. Kristen Gorman, 2007–2009 — 342 penguins with complete bill measurements (274 in the Adelie/Gentoo pair, 219 in Adelie/Chinstrap). Bill length and bill depth in millimetres, shown relative to the average of all 342.
